LET'S BEGIN

Exploring Hagonoy

So, where exactly are we going? Hagonoy is a municipality in Bulacan known for its coastal setting and strong connection to fishing and the water.

As you go around the town, you'll notice that this connection is not just something you hear about. You can actually see it in the way people live, work, celebrate, and create.

01
A town by the water

Fishing and aquaculture remain important parts of Hagonoy's everyday life.

A LITTLE BACKGROUND

A Story That Goes Back

Hagonoy has a long history, and you can still find pieces of that story around the town today.

According to the Provincial Government of Bulacan, Hagonoy became a town in 1771 after being part of Calumpit. But the story of the community goes back even further.

And this is where exploring becomes interesting. Instead of only reading about history, you can actually see reminders of it while walking around Hagonoy.

LOOK CLOSER

Hagonoy Through Art

Now, let's take a closer look at some of the places, structures, and artworks that help tell Hagonoy's story.

01

HISTORIC CHURCH

Saint Anne Parish

Let's start with one of the places you’ll probably notice right away — Saint Anne Parish.

The church has been part of Hagonoy's story for centuries. The National Historical Commission of the Philippines records that the church was established under the Augustinians and was dedicated to Saint Anne.

When you look at the church today, you're not just looking at an old structure. You're also looking at a place that continues to be part of the community.

INSIDE THE CHURCH

A Different View of Saint Anne

Now, take a look inside.

The interior gives you a different view of Saint Anne Parish compared to what you see from the outside. You can also notice the details that make the space feel connected to the church's long history.

It's a small part of the visit, but sometimes looking a little closer is what makes a place more memorable.

02

ANCESTRAL HOUSE

Don Francisco's Ancestral House

Next, let's go to one of Hagonoy's older houses. This is the ancestral house associated with Don Francisco Sebastian.

Local historical accounts describe Don Francisco Sebastian as an important local leader who served as a municipal chief executive during the early 1900s.

The house gives us another way of seeing history. Instead of a monument or marker, we get to see history through an actual home that has remained connected to Hagonoy's past.

03

HERITAGE HOUSE

Casa Damasa — The White House

Here's another house worth noticing. Known as Casa Damasa or The White House, this structure adds another layer to Hagonoy's architectural story.

When you see houses like this, it's easy to think of them as simply old buildings. But look closer, and you'll notice how their design, age, and surroundings help us imagine what life in the community was like before.

04

PUBLIC ART

Mural Painting Street

Now, let's move from old structures to something much more colorful.

The Hagonoy Heritage Mural brings stories and images into a public space where people can actually see them as they go about their day. It is one example of how art can make history easier to notice. Instead of keeping the story inside a book, the mural puts it right where the community can see it.

We also passed by this street during our visit to Hagonoy. However, the water level was quite high because of the high tide at the time, which made it difficult for us to capture clear and presentable photos of the murals ourselves. Because of this, the photographs shown in this part of the website were taken from an external source to give a better view of the street and its murals.

05

MONUMENT

Kol. Santiago Trillana Monument

As we continue around Hagonoy, you'll come across another reminder of the town's historical figures.

The monument is easy to notice because of where it stands along the road. It also gives visitors another reason to pause and think about the people connected to Hagonoy's history.

It's a simple example of how public spaces can keep historical stories visible to the community.

06

HISTORICAL MARKER

Labanan ng Bangkusay

Here's another stop connected to Hagonoy's history. The Labanan ng Bangkusay marker reminds visitors of an important event connected to the area.

Historical markers like this may look simple, but they give a place context. Once you know what the marker is talking about, the surroundings can feel very different.

07

CIVIC HERITAGE

Bahay Pamahalaan ng Hagonoy

And before we leave this part of the journey, let's look at the Bahay Pamahalaan.

Government buildings are also part of a town's heritage because they show how the community developed and organized itself through time.

It may not be an artwork in the traditional sense, but its structure and history are still part of Hagonoy's visual story.

EVERYDAY LIFE

Along the Way

Not every part of Hagonoy's story is found inside a heritage building. Sometimes, you just have to look at everyday places.

COMMUNITY

Hagonoy Public Market

This is where everyday activity becomes part of the experience. The market connects people, food, fishing, and local trade in one busy space.

If you want to get a feel for everyday Hagonoy, this is one of the places to look.

PUBLIC SPACE

Punduhan ng Bayan

The Punduhan ng Bayan is another part of the community landscape. Places like this show that Hagonoy's story is not only about old buildings — it is also about spaces people continue to use.

THE HEART OF EVERYDAY LIFE

Where the Water Meets Life

Now, this is something you can't really separate from Hagonoy — fishing.

The town's location has shaped the way people live and work. Fishing and aquaculture are important parts of Hagonoy's economy and community life.

So when you see fishermen, fish being sold, or boats around the town, you're seeing more than just scenery. You're seeing a part of Hagonoy's identity.

FISHING PART OF HAGONOY'S EVERYDAY LIFE

WHEN THE TOWN CELEBRATES

A Day of Celebration

And of course, we can't finish the journey without seeing how Hagonoy celebrates.

01

Palaisdaan Festival

Held annually every July, the Palaisdaan Festival is a month long celebration that highlights Hagonoy's marine biodiversity, fishing industry, and local culture.

The celebration includes traditional fluvial parades or pagoda along the town's river systems, street dancing, cultural activities, and community gatherings. It also reflects the town's strong connection to fishing and its waters.

02

Barangay and Patronal Feasts

Hagonoy also celebrates different barangay and patronal feasts throughout the year. These celebrations honor the patron saints of different communities and bring people together through faith, tradition, and community gatherings.

Some of the celebrations include the Feast of Saint Anne on July 26, the Feast of St. Michael the Archangel on May 8, the Feast of the Most Holy Rosary during the second week of May and the first Sunday of October, the Feast of Sta. Elena Emperatriz from May 3 to May 4, the Feast of Sts. Peter and Paul on June 29, and the Feast of the Holy Child or Sto. Niño on the third Sunday of January.

LOOKING AHEAD

Hagonoy Today

After seeing all these places, it becomes easier to understand that Hagonoy's identity isn't found in just one landmark.

It's in the old buildings, the murals, the celebrations, the market, the people, and especially in the strong connection between the community and the water.

And that's what makes exploring Hagonoy interesting — there's always another story to notice.
